Twenty years of listening to the land before we speak for it.

Our story

Green Key Resources started in 2004 with a single contract: a hundred-acre maple stand outside Ashford that hadn't seen a management plan in three decades. We walked every acre with the landowner, tallied the timber by hand, and wrote a plan that balanced sugaring income against forty years of growth. That job set the pattern for the next two decades of work.

Today we manage forest health surveys, watershed assessments, and conservation easements across four counties. The tools have changed. We carry GPS units and pull satellite imagery now. The method hasn't: walk the ground, talk to the people who live on it, and write a plan they can actually follow.

Our mission

Working forests that keep working

We help landowners, towns, and land trusts make decisions that keep working forests working. Sustainable consulting means the plan pencils out for the owner and holds up for the watershed twenty years from now. We measure success in healthy stream buffers, diverse stands, and landowners who stay enrolled in their management plans past the first renewal.

How we work

Fieldwork first, spreadsheets second

Every engagement starts with a walk, not a spreadsheet. We cruise timber, map wetlands, and sit at kitchen tables to hear what a piece of land means to the family that owns it. Reports come after the fieldwork, and they're written so a landowner can read them without a forestry degree.

Our team

The people behind the plans

Our staff includes licensed foresters, a wetland ecologist, and a GIS specialist, most of whom grew up within an hour of the office. Between us we hold licenses in Vermont, New Hampshire, and New York, and a combined four decades of fieldwork in the Champlain and Connecticut River watersheds.

"The forests we manage today are the ones our grandchildren will inherit. We treat every acre like it belongs to someone we love."

Mara Whitfield, Founding Forester
